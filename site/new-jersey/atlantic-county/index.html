Can a New Jersey sheriff sale be postponed?

Yes. Under N.J.S.A. 2A:17-36 (as amended by P.L.2019, c.71) the sheriff may make up to five adjournments: two at the lender's request, two at the debtor's request, and one if both agree, each up to 30 calendar days. A court may order more for cause. That is why many listings show several postponements. Source: https://pub.njleg.state.nj.us/Bills/2018/PL19/71_.PDF

How much deposit does the winning bidder pay?

State law (N.J.S.A. 2A:50-64, P.L.2019, c.71) requires the successful bidder to pay a 20 percent deposit, in cash or by certified or cashier's check payable to the county sheriff, immediately when the sale ends. Source: https://pub.njleg.state.nj.us/Bills/2018/PL19/71_.PDF

What happens in the 10 days after the sale?

Under N.J. Court Rule 4:65-5 the sheriff delivers the deed unless a motion objecting to the sale is served within 10 days after the sale (or later, before the deed is delivered). The owner's right to redeem generally runs through that 10-day period, as the Appellate Division explained in MSND Financial v. 187 Loveladies Holdings (2024). Source: https://www.njcourts.gov/system/files/court-opinions/2024/a1744-23.pdf
