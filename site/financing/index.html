How to pay for a sheriff sale

Facts checked as of Oct 26, 2026. Rules change — always confirm with the county.

Disclosure: DeedHound may earn a referral fee from some lenders in the future; not financial advice. DeedHound does not make, arrange or negotiate loans. The links below are plain links — we have no referral relationship with any lender today.

Can you get a regular mortgage for a sheriff sale?

Usually not. The deadlines are short, you can't go inside the house before you buy, and you don't get clear title until the deed. Most buyers pay cash, or line up a short-term "hard money" or bridge loan before the sale. In New Jersey, Somerset County says plainly: "Additional time is not granted to the buyer to obtain a mortgage." (Somerset County Sheriff)

When the money is due

Ohio

Deposit (Ohio Revised Code 2329.211, residential):

  • Appraised $10,000 or less: $2,000
  • $10,001–$200,000: $5,000
  • Over $200,000: $10,000

The court or person running the sale sets when the deposit is due; it is printed in the sale ad. Miss it and the sale is invalid. Online sales may take a card payment. (ORC 2329.211)

Balance: county terms say it is due within 30 days after the court files its confirmation of the sale — not 30 days after the auction. Paying late can cost you the deposit. (Summit County, Clermont County)

New Jersey

Deposit: 20% of your bid at the close of the sale, by certified or cashier's check.

Balance: due within 30 days of the sale. The former owner can redeem during the first 10 days; interest starts on day 11 (Hudson County lists 6.5% for 2026). If you don't pay, the property is sold again and the deposit can be kept.

(Hudson County, Somerset County, Mercer County)

Pennsylvania

Philadelphia & Montgomery: online at Bid4Assets. Send a $10,000 deposit before the auction (Philadelphia tax sales: $1,500). Philadelphia: 10% plus buyer's premium the next business day, balance in 15 days. Montgomery: full balance in 12 days.

Lehigh: in person; 10% by certified check right away, balance in 30 days.

(Philadelphia Sheriff, Montgomery County Sheriff, Lehigh County Sheriff)

Your options, safest first

1. Cash. Simplest and most common. Watch out: it ties up savings in one property.

2. Home equity line (HELOC) or cash-out refinance on a home you already own. Cheaper and fast if set up before the sale. Watch out: your own home secures the loan.

3. Hard money / bridge loan. Short loans (about 6–18 months) for investment property, usually to an LLC. Fast, but expensive, and you usually sign a personal guarantee. One investor guide lists typical Ohio terms of 12–15% interest, 2–4 points and 65–75% of value (AuctionScout, not an official source). Must be lined up before you bid.

4. Pay cash, then borrow against the property. Some buyers pay cash, then take a hard money loan on the property to get cash back (example).

5. Rental (DSCR) loan after you get the deed. A long-term loan approved on the rent rather than your paycheck — handy for retirees, but it can't be used on sale day.

6. Private lender or family. Flexible. Put it in writing and use an attorney.

Checklist before you bid

  1. Have your lender lined up and get proof of funds in writing.
  2. Bring the right deposit (certified/cashier's check in NJ; see the sale notice in Ohio).
  3. Run a title and lien search.
  4. Leave a cushion before the 30-day deadline.
  5. Talk to an attorney — counties recommend it.
